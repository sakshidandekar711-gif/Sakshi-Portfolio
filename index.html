Struvite vs DAP fertiliser: climate impact per kg of phosphorus

openLCA 2.7.0 · ELCD 3.2 · IPCC 2013 GWP 100a · October 2026

1. Goal

Compare the climate impact of phosphorus supplied as struvite recovered from sewage sludge with phosphorus supplied as DAP made from mined phosphate rock. Intended use: portfolio study.

2. Scope

  • Functional unit: 1 kg P delivered, with the same N delivered in both systems (struvite gets an N top-up).
  • Boundary: struvite: chemicals, electricity, heat, packaging, truck transport, diesel, N top-up. DAP: production and sea shipping. Both: N₂O from the N delivered.
  • Not modelled: plant infrastructure, avoided-waste credits, crop-yield differences.
  • Method: ELCD 3.2, EU-27 grid mix, N₂O = 265.

Struvite contributors: N₂O 3.64 (38.8%), N top-up 2.40 (25.5%), electricity 1.66 (17.7%), MgCl₂ 0.87 (9.3%), heat CO₂ 0.87 (9.3%).

5. Interpretation

  • Struvite is 2.94 kg CO₂-eq (about 24%) lower than DAP per kg P.
  • With MgCl₂ at the stoichiometric 3.07 kg per kg P, struvite rises to about 10.06 and stays below DAP.
  • DAP would only tie if its production plus shipping fell to about 5.75 kg CO₂.

6. Limitations and next steps

  • MgCl₂, N fertiliser, DAP production and shipping are stand-ins. DAP is the most uncertain figure.
  • N₂O is identical by design. Only climate change was assessed.
  • Quality check: a stray “container glass” link (total 19.27) was removed, giving 9.39, in line with the Excel model (8.9).
  • Next: cited sources, country-grid sensitivity, more impact categories.

Green vs conventional synthesis: climate impact comparison

LCA (GWP 100) · openLCA 2.4.0, EcoInvent 3.7, ReCiPe Midpoint (H) / IPCC 2013 · October 2026

1. Goal

Compare the carbon footprint of an active chemical product made by green bio-extract synthesis (natural reductants, mild conditions) with the conventional chemical route (synthetic reagents such as NaBH₄). Intended use: portfolio evaluation.

2. Scope

  • Functional unit: 1.0 kg purified active product at equivalent purity.
  • Boundary: gate-to-gate: raw materials and reagents, solvent, heating and stirring electricity, centrifugation, direct off-gas emissions. Excludes end-of-life and downstream packaging.

Inventory values are literature-style stand-ins from bench-scale runs normalised to 1.0 kg yield.

4. Results (kg CO₂-eq per 1.0 kg product)

Green7.65
Conventional22.40
GreenConventional
Total GWP 1007.6522.40
Core chemical footprint5.2516.02

Difference−14.75 kg CO₂-eq (−65.8%)

Green contributors: electricity 31.4%, bio-ethanol 24.8%, biomass extract 21.2%, NaOH 18.6%, transport and others 4.0%.

5. Interpretation

  • The green route is 14.75 kg CO₂-eq (−65.8%) lower per kg of product.
  • The conventional route is burdened by NaBH₄ and DCM incineration (over 40% of its emissions).
  • If ethanol recovery falls from 90% to 60%, the green route rises to 9.20 and keeps a 58.9% advantage.

6. Limitations and next steps

  • NaBH₄ and plant-extract flows rely on literature proxies; replace with primary data.
  • Only GWP 100 was assessed. Ecotoxicity, land use and water depletion are needed to check bio-based trade-offs.
  • Quality check: minor double-counting in wastewater pumping was removed (7.40 Excel vs 7.65 model).

Struvite vs DAP: climate-change comparison in Excel

Excel workbook · sheets: Summary, Inputs, Struvite_calc, DAP_calc, Sensitivity, Notes · preliminary result

Goal and functional unit

Compare struvite (from industrial wastewater, FER-PLAY data) with DAP shipped from Saudi Arabia to Europe. Functional unit: 1 kg phosphorus delivered to farmland, with the same nitrogen supplied by both systems (about 0.90 kg N per kg P). Climate change only, in kg CO₂-eq.

How the model is built

  • Every number sits on an Inputs sheet, colour-coded by reliability: from file, sourced, estimated, placeholder or calculated.
  • Struvite inventory from the FER-PLAY dataset (per tonne), converted to 1 kg P; DAP from its P₂O₅ content, production footprint and sea freight.
  • N₂O uses the IPCC AR6 100-year factor of 273; the nitrogen top-up for struvite is added so both systems supply equal N.

Gulf and Asia scenarios (grid electricity and DAP delivery distance)

The main result uses a European-style grid (0.25 kg CO₂e/kWh). Here the struvite plant’s electricity (3.55 kWh per kg P) is run on national grids in the Gulf and Asia, and DAP is delivered from Saudi Arabia over a shorter or longer sea route. Everything else is unchanged.

  • Struvite stays lower in every scenario. The smallest gap is in Saudi Arabia, where the grid is dirtiest and DAP has almost no shipping.
  • On the model’s numbers, struvite would only lose on a grid above roughly 1.1 kg CO₂e/kWh, higher than any grid in the table.
  • Grid factors: Ember Yearly Electricity Data, 2025 release (full-lifecycle CO₂e, country averages). Sea distances are rounded estimates, not routed distances. DAP production (8.33) is held at the same value everywhere.

Limitations (read before quoting)

  • Climate change only. Eutrophication, energy and other categories are not included.
  • The MgCl₂ carbon factor is a placeholder with no source found.
  • The DAP value comes from one Chinese study; no Saudi-specific data was found.
  • Struvite data are from industrial wastewater, assumed pure; the wastewater treatment itself is outside the boundary.
  • The three regions differ only in field N₂O, so they are not independent measurements.
  • Field N₂O factors (0.010 to 0.018) are European values and were not adjusted for Gulf or Asian soils and climate.

Sources: FER-PLAY struvite inventories (Zenodo, 2025); IPCC AR6; Yara; RSC 2025 supporting information; UK BEIS/Defra via Climatiq; 2025 Chinese DAP carbon-footprint study; Ember Yearly Electricity Data (2025 release) for national grid factors.

Carbon accounting: Scope 1 and 2 for an illustrative chemical plant

Excel workbook · sheets: Input, Factor, Result · GHG Protocol approach · November 2026

Illustrative data. The plant, its fuel and electricity use and its output are invented for practice. This is not a real company’s data and not a verified emissions report.

1. Goal and boundary

Calculate the greenhouse-gas emissions of a small chemical plant making 1,000 tonnes of product per year, and express them per tonne of product. Included: Scope 1 (fuel burned on site) and Scope 2 (purchased electricity), one year, in t CO₂e. Not included: Scope 3, process (reaction) emissions and fugitive leaks.

2. Inputs and emission factors

ActivityAmount per yearScopeEmission factorSource
Diesel500 litres12.66155 kg CO₂e per litreUK DESNZ GHG conversion factors 2026 (100% mineral diesel)
Natural gas120,000 m³12.02633 kg CO₂e per m³UK DESNZ GHG conversion factors 2026
Electricity400,000 kWh20.71 kg CO₂ per kWhCEA CO₂ Baseline Database, Indian grid weighted average, 2025
Production1,000 tonnesUsed for the per-tonne result

3. Method

  • Emissions = activity data × emission factor, in kg CO₂e, then divided by 1,000 to give tonnes.
  • Scope 1 and Scope 2 are summed from the Scope column; the total is divided by annual production for the result per tonne.
  • Three linked sheets (Input, Factor, Result): every result is a formula, so changing an input updates the whole workbook. Each factor lists its source and year.

4. Results (t CO₂e per year)

Electricity284.00
Natural gas243.16
Diesel1.33
MeasureResult
Scope 1 (diesel and natural gas)244.49 t CO₂e
Scope 2 (electricity)284.00 t CO₂e
Total, Scope 1 + 2528.49 t CO₂e
Per tonne of product0.53 t CO₂e per tonne

5. Interpretation

  • Electricity (53.7%) and natural gas (46.0%) make up 99.7% of the total. Diesel contributes about 0.3%.
  • Reducing electricity and gas use would cut emissions most.
  • Sensitivity: a change of 0.10 kg CO₂ per kWh in the grid factor moves the total by about 40 t CO₂e (about 7.6%).

6. Limitations and next steps

  • All plant data are invented. Real work needs metered data and bills, with each source documented.
  • The diesel and gas factors are UK values used as a stand-in for an Indian plant; UK factors are designed for UK activities.
  • The CEA grid factor covers CO₂ only, not CH₄ and N₂O.
  • Process emissions from the chemical reaction itself are not modelled.
  • Next: add two Scope 3 categories (purchased raw materials and their transport), then a CBAM-style embedded-emissions version for a fertiliser.

Sources: UK Government GHG Conversion Factors for Company Reporting 2026 (DESNZ), published 11 June 2026; Central Electricity Authority (India) CO₂ Baseline Database for the Indian Power Sector; GHG Protocol Corporate Standard.
